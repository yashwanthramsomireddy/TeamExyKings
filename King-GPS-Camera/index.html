Built for work in the field

Burned in, not bolted on

The stamp is painted into the pixels at capture. It survives WhatsApp, email, printing and pasting into a report, and it cannot be peeled off afterwards.

Four templates

Classic, Detailed, Compact and Minimal — each positionable in any of the four corners, with adjustable text size and backdrop.

A preview you can trust

The viewfinder overlay is drawn by the same code that writes the saved file. What you line up is what you get.

EXIF as well

Coordinates, altitude and timestamps are written into the file's metadata too, so maps and photo managers can place the shot.

Formats to suit the job

Decimal degrees or degrees-minutes-seconds. Metric or imperial. Five date layouts and a 12- or 24-hour clock.

Your gallery, your files

Photos go straight to Pictures / King GPS Camera, visible to every gallery and file manager on the phone.

Who it is for

  • Site engineers and construction supervisors recording progress
  • Surveyors and inspectors who need a defensible record
  • Property and real-estate agents documenting a listing
  • Field service and maintenance teams closing out a job
  • Anyone who simply wants to remember where a photo was taken

Permissions, plainly

Camera Taking the photo. Required.
Location The coordinates printed on the photo. Optional — decline it and the app still works as a date-and-time stamp camera.
Internet One job only: turning coordinates into a readable street address through Android's own geocoder. Switch off the place name and address in Settings and the app never touches the network.
Storage Saving into your gallery on Android 8 and 9. Not requested on Android 10 and above.

No analytics. No crash reporting. No advertising. No account. Your photos never leave your phone unless you share them yourself.
