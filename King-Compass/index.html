Three tools

Compass

Heading in degrees with the cardinal point, on a dial you can set to rotate the card or just the needle. Live magnetic field strength and sensor accuracy are shown alongside, so you know when to trust the reading.

Bubble level

A two-axis spirit level with pitch and roll to a tenth of a degree. Calibrate it against any surface you know is flat to cancel out a case or an off-square sensor.

Inclinometer

Slope angle from horizontal, with grade percentage and roof pitch in x:12. Hold freezes the reading so you can take the phone off an awkward surface and read it after.

Calibration, explained

  • A step-by-step guide built into the app, with an animated figure-8 to follow.
  • Live accuracy and field-strength readouts so you can see the calibration take effect.
  • Plain-language notes on why a compass drifts and what to do when it still reads wrong.

Earth's magnetic field runs roughly 25–65 µT. If the app reports something far outside that, metal or a magnet nearby is the cause, not the phone.

Built to stay out of the way

No permissions worth the name

Vibration only, for the haptic tick, and you can switch that off. No location, no camera, no storage, no internet.

Entirely offline

Every reading comes from your phone's own sensors. Nothing is uploaded, because the app has no way to reach a network.

True AMOLED black

Pitch black with a white accent. On an OLED screen a black pixel is switched off, which matters for a tool you leave open.

Good to know

  • Headings are magnetic north. True north would need your location, and the app asks for no permissions.
  • Hold the phone flat for an accurate heading — error grows quickly past about 25° of tilt.
  • A few budget phones ship without a magnetometer. The compass says so, and the level and inclinometer carry on working.
  • Android 7.0 (API 24) and above.
