Child Safety Standards

Last updated: October 24, 2026 · DreamLoop 3.1
Zero tolerance. DreamLoop prohibits child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), including child sexual abuse material (CSAM), grooming and sexualised content involving minors. We block it, remove it and report it.

1. How DreamLoop is built to prevent harm

  • The journal is private and stays on the user’s phone. It is not a social network.
  • The only public feature is optional Community, for users aged 18 and over (confirmed before every share).
  • Community is anonymous plain text. There are no profiles, no direct messages, no comments, no images, no links and no way for users to contact each other.
  • No one can find, follow or message a person who shared a dream.

2. How we detect and stop it

  • Automatic screening. Every dream is checked on the phone and again on our server. Sexual content, and any mention of minors alongside sexual or unsafe wording, is rejected. The checks handle disguised spellings.
  • AI safety check. A second check blocks anything uncertain. If it is unavailable, the dream is held back, not published.
  • Human review. Held-back and reported dreams stay hidden until reviewed.
  • Reporting. Every Community dream has a Report button. Three reports from different accounts hide it automatically.
  • Enforcement. Violating content is removed; repeat offenders lose sharing and can be suspended.

4. If we find CSAM

We remove it immediately, stop the account’s access, preserve the information the law requires, and report it to the appropriate authorities — in India through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) and, where relevant, to law enforcement and child-protection bodies elsewhere.

5. Legal compliance

We comply with applicable child-safety laws, including India’s POCSO Act and IT Act reporting duties, and with Google Play’s Child Safety Standards policy.
